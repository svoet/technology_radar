A mainframe exit (or mainframe migration/modernization) is a strategic, multi-year project designed to move workloads, applications, and data off aging legacy mainframe systems (such as IBM z/OS) onto modern platforms, typically cloud-native environments or distributed x86 servers.

Bottom Line Up Front: A mainframe exit is a high-risk, high-cost, and complex IT transformation aimed at eliminating the reliance on legacy COBOL/CICS environments to reduce long-term maintenance fees, talent shortages, and technical debt.
Key Aspects of a Mainframe Exit:
- Goal: Complete decommissioning of the physical mainframe hardware and legacy code, often replaced by microservices on platforms like Google Cloud, AWS, or Azure.
- Challenges: Projects are notoriously difficult and expensive, with Gartner predicting that a high percentage of, specifically 70% of, such projects will struggle or fail by 2030 due to high technical complexity and underestimation of the effort required.
- Process: Instead of a "big bang" approach, successful exits are often done in phases (pilot projects) to systematically de-risk the migration of critical business applications.
- Motivations: Rising maintenance costs, difficulties in recruiting new mainframe staff, and the need for agility to integrate with modern AI-driven solutions.
